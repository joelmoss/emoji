What works

What risks

Most effort to build well and to keep in sync with the real UI as it evolves (glass blur, grid layout, category bar) — a stale-looking mock undercuts the "polished" impression.

Pick this if

What works

Zero decisions to make, zero scrolling — for a tiny, single-purpose utility this matches the product's own philosophy of getting out of your way.

What risks

No room to mention skin tones, recents, keyboard nav, or auto-updates — a visitor comparing against the built-in macOS picker has nothing here to tip the decision.

Pick this if

You want the GitHub repo itself (README) to be the real documentation, and the homepage to be pure "get the file."

How to pick a direction

A one-line rubric for choosing one of these to take forward.

Want it to feel like a polished consumer Mac app →A — Icon-hero
Want to speak to the GitHub/dev crowd directly →B — Feature-grid
Want the product to sell itself visually →C — Live-demo
Want the smallest page that just gets people the .dmg →D — Ultra-minimal
